# Pre-specified amendment MX-1: decision rule, target definition and anticipation in the cue controller

Status: written and committed before any of the controllers in Section 3 were run on any dataset.
The commit time stamp of this file is the reference. The analysis script is committed together with, or after, this file and before it is executed.

## 1. Information available when this amendment was written

Declared so that the analysis plan can be judged against what was known:

1. Outputs of the frozen primary controller (random forest on the Pre-FoG/FoG/Post-FoG union, hysteresis, window-F1 selection with specificity floor 0.70) on DAPHNET, Multimodal FoG and FoG-STAR, including per-window outputs re-generated by `hiss_methodsx_rerun.py` (published values reproduced for all three datasets), the Freeze-index reference, the no-hysteresis ablation, floor and grid sensitivity variants, and matched-false-alarm operating curves (`hiss_methodsx_analyses2.py`, tables A1 to A8).
2. Design-level descriptors of the label structure: recording and stretch durations, FoG prevalence per patient, inter-episode gaps, number of episodes preceded by a clean 4 s window.
3. Pre-FoG versus No-FoG separability (leave-one-subject-out random forest, pooled AUC). Clean Pre-FoG windows with lead of at least 1 s: DAPHNET 0.767, Multimodal FoG 0.570, FoG-STAR 0.617. Last window before onset: 0.829, 0.619, 0.609.

None of the controllers U*, D or A (Section 3) was trained or evaluated before this amendment.

## 2. Rationale

- Patient-wise feature normalisation was rejected before any run: Multimodal FoG and FoG-STAR recordings are short (FoG-STAR 1.4 to 9.5 min per patient) and FoG prevalence reaches 74% of a recording, so running patient statistics would be dominated by FoG windows.
- Under the 0.3 s window-labelling rule, the last window before onset can contain up to 0.25 s of FoG; anticipation is therefore assessed with a minimum lead of 1 s, and the anticipation model is not trained on the last window before onset.
- About one fifth of DAPHNET and Multimodal FoG episodes begin within 4 s of the end of a preceding episode. A cue that remains active from the preceding episode is clinically appropriate but is not counted as timely under the frozen definition; an additional outcome is defined in Section 4 without altering the frozen definition.
- The window-F1 objective of the primary controller is not aligned with event-level goals. The decision rule is therefore replaced by an event-level constrained rule, and its effect is isolated from the effect of the training target (Section 3).
- Separability with lead of at least 1 s is moderate in DAPHNET and close to chance in the two other datasets; anticipation is therefore expected to add value in DAPHNET only.

## 3. Controllers (three-step ablation)

Common to all: leave-one-subject-out; within each training fold, 23 features ranked by mutual information on training patients only (from the same 72 shank features), random forest with balanced class weights (100 trees for the final model, 50 in the inner folds), inner out-of-fold scores from a three-fold subject-grouped split of the training patients. Seed 42 for the primary result.

**Event-level decision rule (used by U*, D, A).** Hysteresis (theta_on 0.30 to 0.90 step 0.05; theta_off 0.10 to theta_on step 0.10) followed by a fixed post-activation hold of 3 s (6 windows, equal to the Post-FoG length): when the hysteresis output switches off, the cue remains active for 6 further windows; if the hysteresis output switches on again during the hold, the hold is cancelled and the cue simply continues. The hold is applied identically during threshold selection and at test. The threshold pair is selected on the pooled inner out-of-fold scores of the training patients. Feasible pairs satisfy both (i) at most 60 false alarms per hour (one per minute, fixed a priori) and (ii) specificity of at least 0.70 on windows outside the false-alarm tolerance zone (the floor of the primary controller). Among feasible pairs, the pair with the highest value of the controller's objective is chosen; ties are broken by fewer false alarms per hour, then by lower time cue on. If no pair is feasible, the pair with the fewest false alarms per hour among those meeting (ii) is taken, and failing that the pair with the fewest false alarms per hour.

- **U\*** (decision-rule effect): training target and score of the primary controller (Pre-FoG, FoG and Post-FoG versus No-FoG); objective: episode coverage.
- **D** (target effect): training target FoG versus all other windows; objective: episode coverage.
- **A** (anticipation effect): detection score of D plus an anticipation score from a second random forest trained on Pre-FoG windows with lead 1 to 4 s (windows 2 to 8 before onset) versus No-FoG windows of the training patients. The two inner out-of-fold scores are combined by a logistic regression fitted on the inner out-of-fold scores (target: FoG or Pre-FoG with lead 1 to 4 s); the fitted combiner is applied to the final models' scores on the test patient. Objective: cue active at onset (Section 4).

Comparator: the frozen primary controller. No parameter other than the threshold pair and the combiner coefficients is learned; no variant other than U*, D and A is run.

## 4. Outcomes

Primary-analysis definitions (episode, coverage, false alarms per hour with the 4 s / 3 s tolerance zone, time cue on, frozen timely activation), plus:

- Timely activation with lead of at least 1 s: cue active in the window before onset and the active run began 1 to 4 s before onset.
- Cue active at onset: timely activation with lead of at least 1 s, or the active run began during a preceding episode or within its 3 s post-episode interval. Because the hold makes the second case more frequent by construction, timely activation with lead of at least 1 s is always reported alongside.

Uncertainty: 5,000-sample patient-level bootstrap of the pooled paired difference between controllers (episode-weighted). Chance reference: 200 circular shifts within each recording stretch. Score quality: for U*, D and A, operating curves (hysteresis threshold sweep with the hold, identical grids) are reported in addition to the deployed operating points; these sweeps use test data and are labelled as operating characteristics, not deployable settings.

Robustness: the full analysis is repeated with seeds 1 to 4 (the seed controls the random forests and the subsample used for mutual-information ranking); the range across the five seeds is reported. False-alarm budgets of 30 and 120 per hour are reported as sensitivity analyses. U* is additionally run without the hold (same selection rule) to separate the effect of the hold from that of the selection rule.

## 5. Hypotheses and decision rules (fixed before running)

Evaluated separately in each dataset; 95% bootstrap CIs are reported without multiplicity adjustment and interpreted per dataset.

- H1 (decision rule): U* has fewer false alarms per hour than the primary controller (CI of the difference below zero) with a coverage loss smaller than 5 percentage points (lower CI bound above -5).
- H2 (target): D has higher coverage than U* (CI above zero) at no more false alarms per hour (upper CI bound of the difference at most +10 per hour).
- H3 (anticipation, DAPHNET): A has higher timely activation with lead of at least 1 s than D (CI above zero).
- H4 (anticipation, Multimodal FoG and FoG-STAR): the difference in timely activation with lead of at least 1 s between A and D lies within plus or minus 5 percentage points (CI within the margin). A CI that is wider than the margin is reported as inconclusive, not as support.

All results are reported whether or not the hypotheses are supported. They are pre-specified secondary analyses of the MethodsX submission and do not replace the frozen primary analysis.
